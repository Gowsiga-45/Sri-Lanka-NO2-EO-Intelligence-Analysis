02 // KEY JUDGMENTS

Analytical Assessment

Judgments integrate satellite-derived observations with supporting open-source evidence. Confidence reflects the strength and consistency of the available evidence.

KJ-01 HIGH CONFIDENCE

Colombo was the dominant persistent NO₂ hotspot.

Colombo recorded the highest district mean tropospheric NO₂ column density and the greatest persistent-hotspot coverage in the district-level analysis.

KJ-02 MODERATE CONFIDENCE

The Western Province pattern is consistent with substantial anthropogenic influence.

EO observations and supporting open-source evidence are consistent with contributions from urban mobility, transportation and industrial activity. Available evidence does not permit reliable attribution to a single emission source.

KJ-03 HIGH CONFIDENCE

The 2020 NO₂ signal was temporally variable.

Independent observations documenting substantial NO₂ reductions during COVID-19 restrictions indicate that changes in human activity materially influenced Colombo's atmospheric NO₂ conditions during the assessment period.

KJ-04 MODERATE CONFIDENCE

Source-specific attribution remains unresolved.

Transport, industrial and other urban emission sources remain plausible contributors. Additional source-specific emissions, meteorological and ground-observation data would be required for stronger attribution.

03 // EO EVIDENCE

Earth Observation Evidence

Sentinel-5P TROPOMI observations were analysed in Google Earth Engine to assess the spatial distribution, temporal behaviour and persistence of tropospheric NO₂ over Sri Lanka during 2020.

EO-01 DIRECT EO EVIDENCE

Colombo recorded the strongest persistent hotspot signal.

District-level analysis identified Colombo as the dominant persistent NO₂ hotspot, with approximately 93.6% persistent-hotspot coverage.

Sensor: Sentinel-5P TROPOMI
Period: January–December 2020
Analysis: Google Earth Engine
EO-02 DIRECT EO EVIDENCE

Gampaha showed substantial persistent hotspot coverage.

Gampaha recorded approximately 59.5% persistent-hotspot coverage, providing additional evidence of a broader elevated NO₂ pattern within western Sri Lanka.

Evidence type: Spatial persistence
Spatial unit: District
Coverage: 59.5%
EO-03 DIRECT EO EVIDENCE

The NO₂ pattern was spatially concentrated.

Persistent hotspot coverage was considerably greater in Colombo and Gampaha than in lower-ranked districts, indicating that the observed signal was not uniformly distributed across Sri Lanka.

Evidence type: Spatial comparison
Interpretation: Geographic concentration
EO-04 ANALYTICAL LIMITATION

Satellite observations alone do not identify individual sources.

TROPOMI observations establish the location and persistence of elevated tropospheric NO₂, but they do not by themselves determine whether an observed signal originated from transport, industrial activity, power generation or another specific emission source.

Implication: Additional evidence required for source attribution.
04 // OSINT

Open-Source Intelligence

Open-source information was reviewed to provide independent context for interpreting the spatial and temporal NO₂ patterns identified in the Sentinel-5P analysis. OSINT evidence is used for corroboration and contextual interpretation rather than direct source attribution.

OSINT-01 HIGH RELEVANCE

COVID-19 restrictions altered urban activity and atmospheric NO₂ conditions.

Independent observations from the 2020 COVID-19 period documented reductions in human mobility and associated changes in atmospheric pollution. This provides external context for interpreting temporal variability in the Sentinel-5P NO₂ signal.

Evidence type: Independent contextual evidence
Supports: KJ-03
Assessment: CORROBORATES
OSINT-02 MODERATE RELEVANCE

Colombo represents Sri Lanka's principal urban and transport concentration.

Open-source information describing population concentration, transportation activity and urban infrastructure in the Colombo metropolitan region provides contextual support for interpreting the geographically concentrated NO₂ pattern observed by Sentinel-5P.

Evidence type: Urban activity context
Supports: KJ-02
Assessment: CONSISTENT WITH
OSINT-03 MODERATE RELEVANCE

Multiple anthropogenic emission sources are plausible within western Sri Lanka.

Transport networks, industrial activity, energy use and other urban processes represent plausible contributors to atmospheric NO₂ in the Colombo–Gampaha region. Their geographic concentration is consistent with the broader western hotspot pattern.

Evidence type: Source-context assessment
Supports: KJ-02 / KJ-04
Assessment: CONTEXTUALIZES
OSINT-04 ANALYTICAL CAUTION

Open-source context does not establish a single emission source.

The presence of transport, industrial and other urban activities near an observed satellite hotspot does not demonstrate that any individual facility or activity caused the measured NO₂ signal. Stronger attribution would require additional source-specific and meteorological evidence.

Evidence type: Attribution constraint
Supports: KJ-04
Assessment: LIMITS ATTRIBUTION

05 // ANALYTICAL SYNTHESIS

Evidence Integration

The assessment distinguishes direct observations from corroborated interpretation and unresolved attribution. This prevents contextual information from being treated as direct evidence of causation.

01 OBSERVED

What does the satellite evidence directly show?

Sentinel-5P observations identify a persistent spatial concentration of tropospheric NO₂ over western Sri Lanka during the 2020 assessment period, with Colombo producing the strongest district-level hotspot signal.

02 CORROBORATED

What is supported by independent information?

Open-source evidence provides independent context indicating that the western region contains substantial concentrations of urban, transportation and economic activity and that changes in human activity during 2020 affected atmospheric pollution conditions.

03 INFERRED

What can reasonably be inferred?

The spatial concentration of the satellite signal together with independent contextual evidence is consistent with a substantial anthropogenic influence on the observed western Sri Lanka NO₂ pattern.

04 UNRESOLVED

What cannot yet be determined?

Available evidence does not permit reliable attribution of the observed NO₂ hotspot to an individual industrial facility, transport corridor, power-generation source or other specific emitter.

ANALYTIC BOTTOM LINE

Persistent NO₂ over western Sri Lanka is consistent with substantial anthropogenic influence, but source-specific attribution remains unresolved.

Confidence is high that Colombo represented the dominant persistent hotspot within the district-level analysis. Confidence is moderate regarding interpretation of the broader western pattern because multiple emission sources and atmospheric processes could contribute to the observed satellite signal.

06 // METHODOLOGY

Analytical Methodology

The assessment combines satellite-derived Earth observation evidence with independently reviewed open-source information using a structured intelligence-analysis workflow.

STEP 01

Define the Intelligence Question

Establish the geographic area, assessment period and analytical question before reviewing explanatory information.

STEP 02

Collect EO Evidence

Sentinel-5P TROPOMI tropospheric NO₂ observations were processed in Google Earth Engine for January–December 2020.

STEP 03

Detect Spatial and Temporal Patterns

Monthly observations were compared to identify recurring hotspots, persistent spatial patterns and district-level differences.

STEP 04

Review Independent OSINT

Open sources were reviewed for information relevant to urban activity, transportation, industrial context and major changes in human activity during the assessment period.

STEP 05

Evaluate Competing Explanations

Candidate explanations were compared against the spatial, temporal and contextual evidence rather than assuming that geographic proximity established causation.

STEP 06

Assign Confidence

Confidence levels reflect the strength, consistency and independence of available evidence and the degree to which alternative explanations remain plausible.

07 // LIMITATIONS

Analytical Limitations

Sentinel-5P measures atmospheric column concentrations rather than emissions directly. Observed NO₂ patterns can be influenced by meteorology, atmospheric transport, retrieval conditions, spatial resolution and temporal sampling.

District-level hotspot statistics simplify spatial variability and should not be interpreted as facility-level measurements.

Open-source evidence provides contextual information but cannot by itself demonstrate that a particular activity caused an observed satellite signal.

Stronger source attribution would require additional evidence such as emissions inventories, meteorological analysis, ground observations, higher-resolution imagery and source-specific temporal information.
